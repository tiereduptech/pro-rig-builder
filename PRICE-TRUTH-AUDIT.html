The finding that costs the most

2,597 rows visible on the site are priced only by a retailer whose prices have not moved in 90 days. Their displayed price has no living source. That is 48% of the live catalog — an order of magnitude more exposure than the 578 identity defects, which are the thing currently getting attention.

What runs, and when it last succeeded

There is no gh CLI in this environment, so “last succeeded” is not read from Actions history. It is read from the evidence a successful run leaves behind: the bot commit it pushes, or the state file it writes. A job that runs green but changes nothing leaves no trace here — and for a price refresher, that is the failure.

Is the verification actually verifying?

For Amazon: yes for price, no for identity — and the two are not connected. verify-catalog-asins.js asks “what does this ASIN cost today?” and answers it well. It never asks “is this ASIN the product this row claims to be.” The identity audit asks exactly that, nightly, and is forbidden from acting on the answer.

On your framing — a confirmed price on a wrong product is worse than an unconfirmed one — the overlap is real, but the raw numbers overstate it in one direction and understate it in another. Here is the join, row by row.

Reading the table

The headline overlap is 334 rows — 11.0% of every confirmed Amazon price sits on a link the audit flagged. But 235 of those are dead ASINs that are already quarantined and invisible to customers, which is the quarantine gate doing its job. The number that describes what a customer can actually reach is 86: rows that are live on the site, carry a “confirmed” price stamp, and are flagged as pointing at the wrong product. Against 2,603 live confirmed rows, that is 3.3%.

But 86 is still too high a number, because the audit over-reports

This is the part worth knowing before anyone acts on the queue. The audit's gate compares the catalog's n field against the Amazon title. The catalog stores the brand separately in b and usually omits it from n — so "O11D MINI V2 Flow | Compact ATX Mid-Tower" is scored against "Lian Li O11D MINI V2 Flow-ATX Mid-Tower PC Case-Black" and fails on missing tokens, despite being the same case.

Re-running the audit's own unmodified gate with the brand restored from b: 44 of the 233 identity findings flip to clean. Narrowing to the 86 live-and-confirmed rows and cross-checking model numbers:

A third failure is hiding inside the second

Testing brand agreement surfaced something the audit is not looking for: 88 of the 233 identity findings have a catalog brand that does not appear in the Amazon title, and many of those are metadata errors, not link errors. The catalog lists b: "AMD" for an Acer Nitro monitor, an ID-COOLING cooler and a PCCOOLER heatsink; b: "MSI" for a UTLGAMENG fan pack; b: "Microsoft" for a Logitech Brio. The links are fine; the brand field is wrong. Because the gate reads brand from the first token of the name and the classifier reads it from b, corrupt brand data both manufactures false defects and would mask real ones.

So the 578 is measuring three different failures at once — dead links, wrong links, and wrong metadata — and reporting them as one rate. The dead-ASIN half is trustworthy and acted on. The identity half is neither.

On the 2,810 figure: the current tree shows 3,036 rows at priceConfidence: "confirmed", of which 2,603 are not quarantined. Neither is 2,810, and no report file in the repo contains that number. It is almost certainly a snapshot from a run a day or two earlier — 2,247 rows were re-confirmed on 08-17 alone, so this count moves by hundreds nightly. The conclusions above do not turn on which figure is used.

What is not working as designed, ranked by cost

Ranked by how many customer-visible rows carry a price with no living source behind it, which is the failure that turns into a wrong click and a lost commission.

  1. Best Buy has no price refresher at all 1,523 rows

    Not broken — absent. No script writes deals.bestbuy.price on any schedule; the catalog verifier is ASIN-keyed and skips the field entirely. One price change across 1,591 series in 90 days. 288 Monitor rows, 186 Storage and 168 Motherboard rows carry Best Buy prices last touched months ago.

    Cost Every Best Buy price on the site is an unverified assertion. Affiliate clicks land on a PDP whose price disagrees with the one that earned the click, which is both the highest-friction way to lose a conversion and the fastest way to lose trust in the comparison itself.

  2. The daily Newegg ingest has never applied an update 2,763 rows

    Running daily since 2026-05-13, 97 consecutive days, zero commits. The feed is fresh and healthy — the watcher measured it at 157 MB with an mtime six hours old. 84% of Newegg price series have never moved. Meanwhile refresh-newegg-prices.cjs, the other path, is correctly disabled for a documented 100% failure rate.

    Cost 1,725 of these rows have no other priced retailer, so this is not a stale-second-opinion problem — for those rows it is the only price on the page. It is also the difference between the pending dead-SKU census being a link cleanup and being a catalog shrink.

  3. Identity defects are detected nightly and acted on never 233 queued

    The audit writes 233 non-dead findings to relink-review-queue.json every night. Its only consumer, quarantine-wrong-product.mjs, is in no workflow. 86 of those rows are live with a confirmed price; roughly 8 are certainly the wrong product and 62 are undecidable without a PDP fetch.

    Cost Smaller than it looks in row count, but it is the failure mode that produces a confidently wrong page — a correct, freshly-confirmed price attached to a product the customer did not search for. The queue growing nightly with nothing draining it also means the number can only rise.

  4. Structured data publishes prices nobody sells at 919 live rows

    ProductSchema computes its price as Math.min(...dealPrices, p.pr), but nothing in the pipeline ever syncs the top-level pr field — the verifier writes deals.amazon.price and leaves pr untouched. Where pr has drifted below every real retailer price, that stale number wins the min and goes into the page's schema.org markup. Example: a fan whose cheapest real price is $23.79 publishes $11.50.

    Cost This is what Google reads for rich snippets. A below-market price in structured data attracts the click and then fails to honour it — the worst possible bounce, and a Merchant-listing quality risk. Note the on-page price is not affected: bestPrice() ignores pr whenever any deal exists.

  5. The displayed price ignores sale prices 618 rendered rows

    bestPrice() reads d.price and never d.saleprice. Newegg feed rows carry both, and where a sale is active the site quotes the regular price — a Thermaltake fan shown at $59.99 that Newegg sells at $49.99, an open-box NZXT fan shown at $33.99 against an actual $13.99. Average overstatement across the 618: $46.34.

    Cost Straight lost conversions: the site makes its own listings look worse than the retailer's, and loses every comparison where the sale price would have won. This is a one-line fix and the cheapest item on this list to close.

  6. Brand attribution is corrupt in the catalog 88 known

    Confirmed cases where b names a manufacturer that has nothing to do with the product: "AMD" on Acer monitors and third-party coolers, "MSI" on a no-name fan pack, "Microsoft" on a Logitech webcam, "Budget" and "GPERHUAN" as brands. 88 surfaced inside the audit findings alone; the true count across 6,938 rows is not measured.

    Cost Mostly indirect, which is why it ranks here rather than higher: it poisons the identity audit in both directions and makes brand filters and facets quietly wrong. It is also a prerequisite for fixing item 3 — the queue cannot be triaged reliably until the brand field can be trusted.

  7. Amazon ingest is manual and has effectively stopped 63 in 30 days

    weekend-amazon-ingest.mjs has no workflow. The main amazon-discovery source last ran 94 days ago. All 63 additions in the last 30 days came from a single hand-run case sweep on 08-07. Against 1,713 new Newegg rows in the same window, the catalog is drifting toward the retailer that cannot price itself.

    Cost No immediate revenue loss — existing rows still reprice correctly. The cost is compounding: every new row lands on a retailer with a frozen price, so the share of the catalog with a living price source falls every time ingest runs.

What is working, and should be left alone

Amazon price verification Four tiers, all succeeding on schedule, 73% of series moving, 98% of confirmations under a week old, two independent sources with a documented fallback.
The dead-ASIN quarantine 345 dead ASINs found, all 345 off the site, zero live. Two-strike rule holding.
The Newegg feed watcher Firing every 30 minutes, correctly measuring the feed's own mtime rather than its memory, currently at zero quiet days.
